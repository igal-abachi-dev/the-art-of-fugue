Generated by `tools/make_docs.py` from `data/work/qa/summary.json` (`python -m artfugue.qa`).

## What was measured, and what was not

**There is no ground truth in numbers.** The acceptance test asked for 30 hand-checked bar/beat positions; none were written
down, so no number here is an error against the truth (the nearest thing is `sync-report.md`, 44 points read off the waveform). The
owner has listened to all the pieces and judged the sync good (`listening-check.md`, 2026-11-06). What is measured:

- **hit30**: fraction of note onsets that have a peak of a short-window (5.8 ms) spectral flux within +-30 ms.
  This signal is independent of the CQT used for the refinement. It is reported for the plain DTW times, for the
  final times, and for the final times shifted by 0.31 s (the "chance" column: how often a peak is there anyway;
  high for the fast pieces IX, XIII, Canon 1 and 4, which have dense onsets, so their evidence is weaker).
- **contrast >= 3 dB**: fraction of notes whose own pitch row gets at least 3 dB louder across the onset.
- **p95 shift**: 95th percentile of how far the refinement moved a note from the DTW time.

## Method

1. Per silence-bounded segment, one DTW (10 frames/s chroma, global, steps (1,1),(1,2),(2,1)) between the score
   rendered as chroma at a nominal tempo and the recording, never over the whole 80 minutes. Memory stays below
   0.5 GB. (`synctoolbox` multi-resolution DTW was not needed at this size.)
2. Pass 1 of the note-level refinement: each note moves to the strongest onset peak (sum of positive level changes
   in the CQT rows of its fundamental and partials) within +-150 ms, if the peak is clear. The running median
   (+-1.5 s) of these shifts corrects the DTW; pass 2 searches +-90 ms around the corrected time.
3. A constant of 19 ms is added to the CQT peaks: measured against the short-window flux it is the same for all
   20 pieces (the CQT flux peaks 19 ms before the attack).
4. Note release = written length x 0.92 (detached touch) from the DTW map. **This is an estimate**, the release was
   not measured.
5. Confidence = half DTW local cost, half peak strength.
6. Velocity = rank of the level right after the onset among all notes of the recording (0.2-1). Its
   correlation with the loudness of 8 s windows is 0.892 (Spearman, 577 windows); the two are
   not independent (both come from the recording's level), so this is a sanity check, not a validation.

## Problem areas (T2.3.3)

- **Repeats**: Canon 1 and 3 are played with their repeats (see `audio-analysis.md`); their bars appear twice in
  `played_bars`.
- **Fermatas and ritardandi**: the last bars of most pieces are slow, as expected (list below: bars longer
  than 1.4x the median bar). Fermata notes get long times (for example Contrapunctus I bar 74: 12.2 s). Their
  confidence is lower because the chroma match is flat during sustained chords.
- **Ornaments** (trills, mordents, turns): flagged in the score data, not expanded. The written note is aligned as one
  event; median confidence 0.4-0.6 for ornamented notes.
- **End of the unfinished fugue**: Contrapunctus XIV ends at 4825.06 s; its last written bar
  (bar 239) sounds at 4822.8-4824.9 s, so the recording stops where Bach's text stops.
- **Canon 2 bars 76-82** and **Canon 3 bars 76-77**: slow and low confidence (final ritardando).

## Hand-set anchors (T2.3.4)

`data/anchors.json` maps a piece to bar times found by listening, for example
`{"c01": [{"bar": 40, "t": 108.2}, {"bar": 40, "pass": 2, "t": 300.0}]}`. An anchored bar gets exactly that
time; the DTW map is shifted so that it passes through the anchors (linear in between, constant before the first
and after the last) before the note refinement runs, so the notes between anchors move too. After every run the
changes against the previous run are printed and written to `data/work/audio/timing_diff.json` (bars that moved
by more than 50 ms). Tried on Contrapunctus I bar 40 (+0.5 s): bar 40 landed exactly on the anchor, the notes of that bar
moved with it, and removing the anchor restored the old times.

## Listening and viewing check

For every piece `data/work/qa/<piece>_<1..3>.wav` (14 s from the beginning, middle and end) mixes the piano with short
tones at the aligned note times (an octave up): a misaligned note is heard as a flam against the piano.
`<piece>_<k>.png` shows the same excerpt as a spectrogram with the aligned notes drawn on it. These files are not
in git (large, regenerated by `python -m artfugue.qa`). **Open:** someone has to listen to them and write down
30 positions (T2.2.1 AC) before the alignment can be called verified.
