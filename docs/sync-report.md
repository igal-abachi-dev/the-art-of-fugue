## What the reference points are, and are not

- 44 read off the **waveform**. "waveform": the instant the level of the recording rises fastest, searched in a window of -0.45 s to +0.3 s around the aligned time, by one of two methods: `broadband` (the whole band, 150 Hz to 6 kHz, for a note that stands alone: everything before it ended at least 0.6 s earlier and the next note is at least 0.45 s later) or `partials` (the level at the note's own pitch and its first partials, for a note no other note begins within 0.2 s of, and none within 0.4 s at a pitch that would blur with it). "ear": a person listened and wrote the time down.
- The waveform points do not use the alignment's own signals, but they are **not a person's ear**, and they are **isolated attacks only**: entries after a rest, piece beginnings, notes after a fermata. The dense polyphony in between is covered by no reference point; for that the evidence is the onset hit rates in `alignment-report.md` and looking at `/stage?spectro`. Points are skipped only where the recording has no clear attack (a rise under 14 dB) or two comparable ones; a point where the alignment is far off is *not* skipped, it counts as the error it is.
- To add points that count more, listen at a place in the spectrogram view and append `{"piece": ..., "tick": ..., "pitch": ..., "voice": ..., "pass": 1, "t_ref": <seconds>, "source": "ear", "kind": ..., "bar": ...}` to `data/sync-reference.json`; they are kept when the points are measured again.
- A person has also listened: on 2026-11-06 the owner listened to all pieces and judged the sync good (`listening-check.md`). That report has no numbers and wrote no reference point, so it is not part of the figures above.
- The error is the shipped onset minus the reference, in the recording's own timeline. It does not include the delay between the clock and what comes out of the loudspeaker (output latency: not calibrated, see `playback.md`).
